The Problem & The Fix

Why Routine Tankless Flushing Matters

Minerals in the water can accumulate inside a tankless heat exchanger over time. The rate varies with local water conditions, treatment, usage, and the unit itself. Here's what routine descaling addresses.

Without Appropriate Maintenance

Hard-water minerals such as calcium and magnesium can accumulate inside the heat exchanger. Without appropriate maintenance, that scale may become more restrictive over time.

  • Reduced hot water flow & pressure
  • Higher energy bills from lost efficiency
  • Strange popping or rumbling noises
  • Recommended maintenance may be missed
  • Increased risk of scale-related performance problems
  • Premature system replacement

With Routine Flushing

A professional descaling flush removes mineral buildup from the water circuit and helps keep routine maintenance current.

  • Mineral scale removed from the water circuit
  • Inlet filter cleaned when accessible
  • Flow and restart checked after service
  • Completed maintenance documented
  • Scale-related restrictions may improve
  • Supports more reliable long-term operation

The Service

What Happens During a Flush

A complete service visit takes about 1–1.5 hours. Here's the process I run on every unit.

1

System Inspection

I check the brand, model, and connections, look for signs of leaks or corrosion that could indicate a slow leak, and assess the degree of mineral buildup before starting.

2

Setup & Isolation

I shut off the gas and water supply, connect to the unit's isolation valves, and stage the descaling pump, hoses, and reservoir for a clean, contained flush.

3

Heat Exchanger Descaling

I circulate the manufacturer-appropriate descaling medium through the heat exchanger for the time specified by the applicable unit and product instructions. Some manufacturers specify white vinegar; others permit an approved descaler.

4

Inlet Filter Cleaning

I remove and clean the cold water inlet filter to restore full flow into the unit. If the filter is damaged, replacement is available for an additional charge.

5

Rinse & Restart

I flush remaining descaling solution from the system, restart the unit, confirm proper ignition, and verify temperature output at the fixture.

6

Service Documentation

You receive an emailed receipt detailing the work performed — useful for your records, manufacturer warranty claims, and future maintenance scheduling.

Professional-Grade Service

What a Proper Flush Requires

Some homeowners can perform routine flushing when the unit has service valves and they follow the exact manufacturer instructions. Professional service is useful when access, the model-specific procedure, filter service, or the shutdown and restart sequence is uncertain.

Submersible Descaling Pump

A recirculating pump sized for the procedure moves the selected descaling medium through the heat exchanger in a controlled loop.

5/8" Service Hoses

Heat-rated lines with proper fittings to connect securely to your unit's isolation valves without leaks or pressure loss.

Manufacturer-Appropriate Descaling Medium

Rinnai's published procedure specifies undiluted white vinegar, while other manufacturers may permit an approved descaler. The unit's model instructions control the method used.

Filter Service Tools

Specialized tools for safe inlet filter removal without damaging the manifold or cross-threading the housing on reinstall.

Beyond equipment: a proper flush requires knowing how to safely isolate and depressurize the system, circulate and rinse the selected medium, service the inlet filter, and restart the unit in the correct sequence. Manufacturer instructions and warranty terms vary by brand and model.

Warning Signs

Signs Your Tankless Water Heater Needs Flushing

If you notice any of these issues, your tankless system may be overdue for cleaning, descaling, or annual maintenance.

  • Hot water is not getting as hot as it used to
  • Reduced hot water pressure or flow
  • Longer wait times for hot water
  • Fluctuating water temperature
  • Error codes or shutdowns
  • Popping, rumbling, or unusual noises
  • It has been more than 12 months since the last flush
  • The inlet filter shows mineral buildup

Rinnai Error Code Help in Irvine

A tankless error code does not always mean the unit needs replacement—or that a flush is automatically the answer. Dustin diagnoses and repairs Rinnai tankless systems, including current service focus on Code 11 and Code 17.

Tankless Repair & No-Hot-Water Service

Rinnai Code 11

Code 11 generally indicates that the unit attempted to ignite but did not establish a flame. Diagnosis may involve the gas supply, ignition and flame-sensing components, venting, wiring, or controls.

Code 11 Diagnosis & Repair

Rinnai Code 17

On applicable Rinnai SENSEI models, Code 17 commonly means debris is blocking the venturi. Service can include removing and cleaning the venturi assembly, reinstalling it, and completing the applicable reset procedure.

How do I know if my tankless needs flushing?

Common signs include lukewarm water that used to be hot, longer wait times at the tap, unusual noises, reduced flow, or visible mineral buildup around the unit's valves. Error codes and complete loss of hot water should be diagnosed because a flush is not the correct repair for every fault.

How often should I flush my tankless water heater?

About once a year is a common starting point, but the correct interval depends on the manufacturer, model, water hardness, water treatment, and household usage. Follow the unit's manual and shorten the interval when its guidance or water conditions call for it.

My unit is only a year or two old — do I really need this?

Possibly. Maintenance needs begin with the manufacturer's instructions and local water conditions rather than the age of the unit alone. Irvine Ranch Water District says hardness varies by source, location, and season, so review the manual and service history instead of assuming a newer unit needs no maintenance.

Repairs, diagnosis, parts, missing or stuck service valves, and unusual access are separate.

How long does a flush take?

About 1 to 1.5 hours for a standard accessible unit. Circulation and rinse time follow the applicable manufacturer and product instructions.

Can I flush my tankless myself?

Ultra Home Repair's $150 professional flush is often more practical than buying equipment for a DIY service. If you choose to do it yourself, follow your manufacturer's instructions carefully to avoid damaging the unit or affecting warranty coverage.

Do I still need to flush my tankless water heater if I have a water softener?

A properly operating water softener can reduce scale, but it does not automatically eliminate the manufacturer's maintenance requirements. The appropriate interval depends on the unit, actual water hardness, household usage, and whether the softener has operated continuously.

A unit with no hot water or an error code should be diagnosed before assuming a flush will fix it. Descaling may help when water-side scale is contributing to the problem, but it will not correct unrelated ignition, venting, combustion, sensor, control-board, or component faults.
